Computer security can be defined by the following CIA properties.

**Confidentiality**
- _Data confidentiality_: confidential information is not disclosed to unauthorized individuals
- _Privacy_: individuals control what information related to them may be collected and stored and by/to whom that information may be disclosed

**Integrity**
- _Data integrity_: information and programs are changed only in a specified and authorized manner
- _System integrity_: a system performs its intended function, free from unauthorized manipulation

**Availability**
- _Systems work promptly_ and services are not denied to authorized users

Additional properties are:
**Authenticity**
- _Identification_: the possibility of correctly identifying an entity
- _Message authentication_: confidence in the validity of a transmission, a message, or message originator/receiver

**Accountability**
- The possibility of tracing an event to a _unique entity_

### Impact on systems
When security is violated the **impact on systems** is measured in:
- _Effectiveness_ of primary functions is reduced
- _Damage_ to organizational assets
- _Financial_ loss
- _Harm_ to individuals

### Keys security concepts
- **Asset**: anything valuable that needs protection
- **Security policy**: rules defining activities to protect assets
- **Threat**: circumstance that could harm an asset
- **Vulnerability**: a weakness that can be exploited
- **Adversary**: individual that carries harmful activities
- **Attack**: a threat put into action, often by exploiting an action
- **Countermeasure**: a technique that reduces the likelihood or impact of an attack
- **Risk**: a measure of potential harm based on its likelihood and impact

### Classifying attacks
- **Passive attack**: observes or collects information without altering resources
- **Active attack**: alters resources or disrupts operations
- **Outside attack**: originates outside the security perimeter (e.g. bypass access)
- **Inside attack**: originate within the security perimeter (e.g. abuses privileges)
